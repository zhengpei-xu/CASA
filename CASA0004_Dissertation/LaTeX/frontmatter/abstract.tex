\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}
%TC:ignore
The most comprehensive national design standard for cycling infrastructure in England, Local Transport Note 1/20 \parencite{departmentfortransport2020a}, is anchored in the principle cycling should be accessible to everyone. Its evaluation instruments, however, were conceived for scheme-level auditing and cannot assess whether compliant schemes cohere into a network navigable at tolerable stress levels for cyclists. This dissertation develops a Level of Traffic Stress (LTS) classification derived entirely from LTN~1/20's design criteria, producing the first LTS framework calibrated to English design standards, and operationalises it using the newly released Ordnance Survey Cycling Lane data. The classification incorporates directed junction assessment grounded in LTN~1/20's Junction Assessment Tool, with bearing-based conflict detection and roundabout-specific criteria absent from prior LTS implementations. Applied to the West Midlands, the English region with the highest share of private motorised trips and a cycling mode share of 0.9\%, the framework reveals a high proportion of low-stress roads does not constitute a usable network. Low-stress segments comprise over three-quarters of network length yet fragment into thousands of disconnected components: high-stress arterial roads sever the low-stress fabric, junction stress concentrating where residential clusters must cross them and roundabouts acting as high-stress barriers. Fragmentation collapses low-stress network reach to the immediate neighbourhood, and origin--destination connectivity collapses with it: only 4.1\% of residents can reach their nearest school at conditions suitable for children, and only 13.0\% of commuting flows within 5 miles are connected at stress levels tolerable to mainstream adults, with connected pairs facing a median detour of 1.76 times the shortest path. A growth simulation shows upgrade sequencing matters as much as volume: component-bridging strategies reach connectivity milestones in roughly half the upgrade length of random selection. With nationally consistent thresholds and data, the framework enables systematic, comparable evaluation of any English cycling network against a single statutory standard.
