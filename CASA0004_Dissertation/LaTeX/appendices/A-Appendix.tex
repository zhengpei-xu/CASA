\section{Traffic Volume Prediction}
\label{app:traffic-volume}

OS~NGD Transport does not include traffic volume for minor roads, so AADT is estimated for all English minor roads following the routing-importance method of \parencite{morley2016} and joined to the OS~NGD network; major roads use observed DfT AADT directly. Routing importance is derived by partitioning the England OSM network into zones bounded by major roads (via pgRouting on a PostGIS topology of 2,996,810 segments) and running one-to-many Dijkstra from each zone's boundary entry points to every internal minor-road endpoint, standardised by the number of minor edges in the zone.

A Poisson GLM with log link relates observed AADT to routing importance and contextual predictors:

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:aadt-glm}
\log \mathbb{E}[\text{AADT}_i] 
= \beta_0 
+ \beta_1 \log(1 + I_i) 
+ \beta_2 \log(1 + A^{\text{major}}_i) 
+ \beta_3 \mathbb{1}_{\text{urban}}(i) 
+ \sum_{c \in C} \gamma_c \mathbb{1}_{c}(i),
\end{equation}

\noindent where $I_i$ is the routing importance of link $i$; $A^{\text{major}}_i$ is the observed AADT at the nearest DfT major road count point; $\mathbb{1}_{\text{urban}}(i)$ is a binary indicator from the ONS 2021 OA Rural--Urban Classification; and $\{\mathbb{1}_c(i)\}$ are dummies for OSM road class $c \in \{\text{secondary, tertiary, residential, unclassified}\}$.

Trained on 3,695 DfT AADT minor road count points (categories MCU and MB), five-fold cross-validation yields $R^2 = 0.604 \pm 0.019$ and Spearman's $\rho = 0.803 \pm 0.015$, comparable to \parencite{morley2016}. Rank-order accuracy is the relevant metric because LTS classification depends on AADT thresholds rather than exact volumes. West Midlands predictions are joined to OS~NGD Road Links via nearest-network spatial join on midpoints; unmatched links (35,815 of 444,016, 8.1\%) are imputed by the median predicted AADT of their OS~NGD road classification (Fig.~\ref{fig:aadt-sources}). The resulting AADT surface (Fig.~\ref{fig:aadt-final}) shows the expected pattern of high volumes on the arterial grid decaying into low-volume residential interiors.

\section{LTN 1/20 CLoS Web Assessment Tool for the West Midlands}
\label{app:clos-tool}

As a second deliverable of this study developed in collaboration with Transport for West Midlands, an interactive web-based CLoS Assessment Tool has been produced and is publicly accessible at \url{https://zhengpei-xu.github.io/ltn1-20-assessment-tool-wm/web/} (Fig.~\ref{fig:web-tool}). The tool provides a map-based interface that allows practitioners to explore Cycling Level of Service scores derived from LTN~1/20 criteria across the entire West Midlands Combined Authority road network. CLoS scoring is pre-computed for 183,923 individual road links, providing a consistent, network-wide baseline that has not previously been available at this scale. Users can click any two points to generate the lowest-stress route between them, view directional CLoS scores broken down by the five LTN~1/20 principles (Safety, Attractiveness, Comfort, Directness, Coherence), and inspect link-level details including detour ratio, gradient and network length.

The tool is designed for three groups of users. Scheme promoters and feasibility teams in the seven metropolitan boroughs can use it for early-stage desktop screening, identifying problem corridors before committing budget to site surveys. TfWM strategy and data teams can use it as a shared quality baseline for cross-authority comparison and for monitoring change over time as new schemes are delivered. Assessors preparing formal CLoS audits for Active Travel England's Route Check process can use it to pre-screen corridors so that site effort is focused where it is most needed. Its advantage over existing audit tools is scale and consistency: rather than assessing one scheme at a time by hand, the tool applies uniform, LTN~1/20-anchored criteria to every link in the region, enabling comparable evaluation across boroughs, corridors and time periods within a single interface.
